# Real stories behind the "make it sell or keep it true" question

Purpose: genuine, checkable cases (not invented scenarios) of African creatives who chose passion and control, and of those who built for sustainability, with what each choice gave and cost. For the stage after the room votes, and for Day 3. Nothing here is on a phone screen yet.

**Status:** researched from public sources in this session. Every claim must be re-checked against the source before it goes on a screen or into a speech. Where sources disagree, it is flagged. These are my pairings and readings, not established findings.

## Passion and control first

**Ousmane Sembène (Senegal), filmmaker**
- Gave: films made on his own terms; widely called the "father of African cinema".
- Cost: sources describe financing as hard and unpredictable throughout his career; one scholarly excerpt says a French ministry film bureau turned down a script, and he cut the film to one hour to meet a funder's rules (re-check this one against the original); he said he was ready to "sleep with the devil or she-devil" to make his films (also re-check); distribution to African audiences was a lifelong struggle.
- Sources: [Walker Art Center](https://walkerart.org/magazine/the-man-behind-the-legend-the-story-of-ousmane-sembene), [BFI interview](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/bfb-archive-revisted-ousmane-sembene-state-african-cinema), [Harvard Film Archive](https://harvardfilmarchive.org/programs/ousmane-sembene-cinematic-revolutionary-2)

**Fela Kuti (Nigeria), musician** (sensitive: handle with care)
- Gave: full control of his music and a global legacy.
- Cost: per one source, he refused to perform outside Africa for much of the 1970s; after the February 1977 raid on his compound and contract disputes he was financially strained and toured Europe. His mother died of injuries from the raid (do not use this casually). One source dates a separate raid to 1974; another site gives the 1977 raid as 1978: use only what you have checked.
- Source: [OkayAfrica](https://www.okayafrica.com/how-20-months-in-prison-redefined-fela-kutis-music/1414925)

**El Anatsui (Ghana-born, Nigeria-based), sculptor** (a third path, my reading)
- Taught sculpture at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka from 1975 until about 2011 (sources differ slightly on the length). In the 1990 Venice Biennale he was not well known outside Africa; the large breakthrough came in 2007; Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement in 2015.
- Reading: a long salaried job may have paid for decades of patient practice, so the art did not have to earn. The sources state the dates, not this interpretation.
- Sources: [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/El-Anatsui), [La Biennale](https://labiennale.org/en/node/2238)

## Built for sustainability first

**Nollywood's origin (Nigeria)**
- Gave: widely credited with starting the home-video boom. Kenneth Nnebue, who imported blank video cassettes, shot *Living in Bondage* (1992) straight to video. A reported cost of about 150,000 naira (about US$7,000) is journalistic and approximate.
- Cost: sources describe budgets as small as US$15,000 and filming in under two months; piracy is blamed for most of the lost income (one industry piece says close to 80% of profit margins in the video era, others say over 70% of DVD sales; these are estimates from sources with an interest, not audited); a small study found most low-budget producers about broke even.
- Disputed: whether *Living in Bondage* really started it, and its sales (reports range from 100,000 to over a million copies). Do not state sales or "first".
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_in_Bondage), [Guardian Nigeria](https://m.guardian.ng/sunday-magazine/awaiting-second-coming-of-living-in-bondage/), [WIPO Magazine](https://www.wipo.int/web/wipo-magazine/articles/the-nollywood-phenomenon-35747)

**Mavin (Nigeria), label**
- Gave: founded in Lagos in 2012 by Don Jazzy; took outside investment in 2019 (Kupanda Holdings) and Universal Music Group announced a majority investment in Mavin Global in February 2024 (announced, subject to approval: do not say "bought").
- Not established by these sources: that Mavin was "built as a business" or what it cost the music. Use it only as an example of commercial structure attracting capital.
- Sources: [Quartz](https://qz.com/africa/1539118/don-jazzys-mavin-gets-multimillion-dollar-investment), [UMG release](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/umg-announces-majority-investment-in-iconic-afrobeats-label-mavin-global-accelerating-expansion-of-africas-outstanding-creative-platform-302071077.html)
